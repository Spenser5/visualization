**What software did you use to create your data visualization?**
Python

**Who is your intended audience?**
Those involved in creating, planning, and funding drop-in activity programs in Toronto. 
    
**What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization?**
I am trying to show how many people attending drop-in programs in Toronto each year, and more specifically each season. That way attendance can be judged over time (based on year to year attendance) and within each specific year (based on the distribution of attendance by season).

    
**What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots?**

Aesthetic: I think the figure is pleasant to look at. I worked at dividing each bar by season for each comparisons. The visualization is substantive in that it accurately represents the data, especially since 2015 fall data was missing I didnt want it too look misrepresented in that year so I only included seasonal data from complete years (2009-2014). I think the figure is also perceptual as I think it is clear what the purpose of the visualization is: to show attendence by year and by season. 
    
**How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization?** 
I used python code and an open source data set, this should be reproducible. One could follow my comments/code and create the same figure. 

**How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?**  
I tried to make the figure as accessible as possible. I myself am colorblind so I chose colours that I could distinguish clearly when next to eachother for this figure. 
    
**Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?**
People planning drop-in programs might see the distribution by season as advice as to when to promote the programs more or even when to cut programs if attendance is low enough. Any changes in programs due to the visualization would impact the public, i.e. people who use the drop-in programs. 

**How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization?**
I chose one table from the dataset that I thought had a clear use and message. Keeping it simple is helpful in viewers fully understanding the visualization. I did not want to add more info and over-complicate the message.
